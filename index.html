3Threat landscape

Who is attacking, how they get in, and what changed structurally. German/EU lens throughout.

Ransomware: consolidation, data extortion, and increased targeting of Germany

The ecosystem is re-concentrating: active groups fell from 85 to 71 over two quarters and the top 10 now account for 71% of victims.[17] Volume remains near record highs (Q1 2026: 2,122 listed victims, second-highest Q1 ever).[16] The underlying economics are shifting — payment rate hit a record-low ~28% and total payments declined slightly (~$820M, −8% vs. 2024) despite ~50% more attacks, pushing groups toward fewer, bigger targets (median payment jumped to ~$60k) and toward pure data-theft extortion without encryption.[13, 14] Backups and hypervisors are now deliberately targeted to deny recovery.[12]

For Germany specifically: BSI counted 950 ransomware cases (~80% SMEs) in its 2025 reporting period, and Bitkom puts total cyber damage to the German economy at a record ~€202bn.[18, 19] SafePay directed more than half its European activity at German organizations.[5]

Initial access: exploitation leads; voice-based social engineering rising

Vulnerability exploitation is now the #1 initial access vector in both major datasets — Mandiant M-Trends (32% of intrusions, 6th straight year) and, for the first time in 19 years, Verizon DBIR, where it overtook credential abuse.[12, 22] The exploited surface is overwhelmingly the network edge: VPNs, firewalls, gateways, MFT servers and hypervisors.

Vishing is the fastest-rising vector (11% of intrusions, now #2 in M-Trends). Help-desk impersonation — the Scattered Spider / ShinyHunters playbook — drove the largest breaches of the period. Fake-CAPTCHA “ClickFix” lures grew +563%. Email phishing is declining in relative terms; voice and SMS now outperform it.[11, 12]

The attack chain has industrialized. Median time from initial compromise to hand-off to a second criminal team collapsed from 8+ hours (2022) to 22 seconds. Initial-access prices fell ~70% (to ~$439). 82% of detections involve no malware at all — stolen sessions, legitimate tools, hands-on-keyboard. Infostealer-leaked credentials precede ransomware by a median of 95 days, and third-party involvement in breaches grew +60% to nearly half.[13, 22, 23]

SaaS/OAuth integrations became a primary vector — the Salesloft Drift token theft cascaded into ~700 Salesforce customers (Cloudflare, Palo Alto, Zscaler among them) and set the template ShinyHunters ran throughout H1 2026 (Instructure/Canvas ~275M records, One Medical 8.8 TB, MSG, Charter, Match Group via AppsFlyer — data volumes partly attacker-claimed).[24, 25]

4Vulnerability & exploitation trends

What is actually being exploited — and why patch-cycle SLAs alone no longer work.

Exploitation now outpaces remediation

CVE volume keeps setting records (BSI: 119 new vulnerabilities/day, +24% y/y; NIST enriched 42,000 CVEs in 2025) while exploitation gets faster: ~29% of newly exploited CVEs are exploited on or before disclosure day, and Mandiant's mean time-to-exploit is now −7 days — before a patch exists. Meanwhile the median enterprise takes 43 days to patch a KEV. Only ~1% of CVEs are ever weaponized — prioritization by exploitation evidence (KEV/EPSS) is the only sustainable model, which CISA has now codified (BOD 26-04).[7, 18, 35, 36]

Supply chain: sustained attacks on open-source registries

The Shai-Hulud worm (Sep + Nov 2025) trojanized ~700 npm packages and created 25,000+ malicious GitHub repos, harvesting cloud and CI/CD credentials at ecosystem scale — the objective has shifted from data theft to pipeline and cloud credential compromise. Follow-on worms (TanStack +160 packages) and multi-registry campaigns (npm/PyPI/Docker Hub in 48 hours) continued into 2026. Combined with the Drift OAuth cascade and third-party involvement in ~half of breaches (DBIR), supplier and integration risk is now a top-3 exposure for most organizations.[22, 45, 46]

Structural changes in the vulnerability ecosystem

NVD is no longer a complete enrichment source — NIST formally de-prioritized everything outside KEV entries and US-federal-relevant software (Apr 2026); pre-March-2026 backlog moved to “Not Scheduled.” Supplement with KEV, EPSS, CNA scores and vendor data. CVE program funding is now durable (protected CISA budget line after the 2025 scare). ENISA's EUVD is becoming the EU-relevant layer, and will absorb CRA mandatory exploited-vuln reporting from Sept 2026 — expect EU-specific disclosure volume to rise. Secure-by-design pressure is shifting from US voluntary pledges to EU law (CRA, PLD).[35, 47, 48]

Watch: EU cybersecurity package

The Commission's Jan 2026 package (CSA 2.0 + targeted NIS2 amendment) would relieve ~28,700 smaller companies from NIS2 scope, create single-entry incident reporting, a ransomware helpdesk, and an expanded operational role for ENISA (including running the EU Cybersecurity Reserve established by the 2025 Cyber Solidarity Act), and an ICT supply-chain framework against high-risk vendors. Legislative negotiations continue through 2026 — no re-planning is required yet, but scope changes should be tracked.[58]

Watch: DORA enforcement

Supervisors moved from tolerance to active enforcement in 2026: registers of information are cross-checked EU-wide, first TLPT cycles are running, and critical-provider oversight (penalties up to 1% of daily worldwide turnover) is live. No headline fine yet — one is likely within the prediction window (P9).[59]

Watch: Chat Control

The voluntary-scanning derogation expired 3 April 2026; trilogues on the permanent CSA Regulation aimed for political agreement in July 2026. Client-side scanning vs. E2E encryption remains the disputed core — relevant to any org relying on E2E-encrypted messaging. Outcome open as of this edition.[60]

6Technology shifts

What's real, what's early, and where the market is moving.

AI in the SOC: maturing capability; plan for augmentation

Every major platform now ships agentic SOC capability (Microsoft Security Copilot agents, CrowdStrike Charlotte AgentWorks, SentinelOne Purple AI agentic investigations — now GA for all customers, Google SecOps agents). Vendor-claimed outcomes are substantial (Microsoft: 75% of phishing/malware investigations automated in internal testing); independent assessments are more conservative: Gartner places actual AI-SOC adoption at 1–5%, typical production use is alert enrichment and report drafting with humans retaining decision authority, and stalled pilots are common.[64, 65, 66]

Assessment: the capability trajectory is credible — Google Big Sleep's SQLite finding pre-empted active exploitation. Establish the data foundation and evaluation criteria now; procure against measured outcomes rather than demonstrations; retain human approval on containment decisions through 2027.[67]

Securing AI: standards emerging for a new attack surface

2025–26 delivered the canonical incidents (EchoLeak zero-click prompt injection; Drift OAuth cascade; GTG-1002) and the first serious guidance wave: OWASP Agentic Top 10 (2026), NSA's MCP security sheet (Jun 2026), NIST's COSAIS control overlays, ISO 42001 as the emerging governance certificate. Every AI agent is a new identity with tool access; prompt injection is unsolved by design. Where copilots and agents are deployed: maintain an inventory, scope permissions as for privileged service accounts, and log tool invocations.[32, 68, 69, 70]

Post-quantum: transport layer largely migrated; the remainder is not

Hybrid PQC key exchange (X25519MLKEM768) now covers ~two-thirds of browser traffic and ~6M Cloudflare domains by default; OpenSSH and Windows ship PQC natively. The hard part — certificates/signatures, PKI, code signing, IPsec, OT/legacy — has barely started. With BSI's hard deadlines (classical-only key exchange dead after 2031, high-protection uses after 2030) and harvest-now-decrypt-later as standing rationale, a cryptographic inventory (CBOM) in 2026–27 is the prudent first step.[62, 71]

Identity: the primary control plane — increasingly non-human

Passkeys reached ~5bn in use; 68% of organizations are deploying them for workforce sign-in — but only 28% are fully passwordless, and attackers responded by stealing sessions instead of passwords (infostealer → token replay → MFA bypass). Machine identities outnumber humans by estimates ranging from 45:1 to over 100:1 (Palo Alto/CyberArk's 2026 report: 109:1), and AI agents raise the count further — the thesis behind Palo Alto's completed $25B CyberArk acquisition ("secure every identity — human, machine, agentic"). ITDR and non-human identity governance are the fastest-growing control categories. Market backdrop: consolidation accelerating (Google completed its $32B Wiz acquisition in March 2026), security spend growing ~14% to ~$244bn in 2026.[72, 73, 74, 75]

83-year outlook to mid-2029 · revised quarterly

Strategic themes, not predictions — the positions that should inform architecture and budget decisions. Deliberately stable: this section changes only when the strategic picture does. Next scheduled revision: October 2026.

T1 · Machine-speed operations

Attack chains already run at 22-second hand-offs and 29-minute breakouts; agentic tooling will push routine intrusion work toward full automation on both sides. By 2029, detection-to-containment SLAs measured in minutes become the operating standard, and the meaningful differentiator between defenders is not budget but automation readiness — clean telemetry, decision playbooks a machine can execute, humans reserved for judgment.

Position: invest in the SOC data foundation and response automation now, even while AI-SOC products are immature. The binding constraint in 2028 will be data quality, not vendor capability.

T2 · Identity becomes the perimeter — including non-human

Passwordless becomes the workforce default; attackers complete their pivot to sessions, tokens, OAuth grants and machine identities. Every AI agent is a privileged identity; NHI-to-human ratios keep climbing. Expect identity infrastructure to consolidate into platforms (the CyberArk/Palo Alto thesis) and ITDR to fold into standard SOC scope.

Position: a single identity security program covering humans, workloads and AI agents — inventory, least privilege, credential lifecycle, session binding — will outperform any set of point solutions. Universal phishing-resistant MFA is the 2026 baseline, not the target state.

T3 · Liability shifts to software producers

CRA full application (Dec 2027) plus the new Product Liability Directive make secure-by-design a market-access and liability question in the EU. SBOMs, declared support periods and exploited-vuln reporting become procurement standard; slow vendor patching becomes a contractual and liability matter rather than an internal risk acceptance.

Position: incorporate CRA-grade requirements into supplier contracts from 2026; where the organization ships products with digital elements, treat CRA conformity as a 2026–27 program rather than a reactive 2027 effort.

T4 · The crypto-agility decade begins

BSI's 2030/2031 deadlines and the EU 2030 critical-infrastructure target make 2027–28 the realistic window for inventory and migration planning — long-lived sensitive data is already exposed to harvest-now-decrypt-later. Signature/PKI migration will be slower and messier than the TLS layer was.

Position: cryptographic inventory (CBOM) 2026–27, migration plan by 2028, and crypto-agility (algorithm replacement without re-architecture) as a design requirement for all new systems from now on.

T5 · Geopolitical sabotage risk as a standing planning assumption

Chinese OT pre-positioning (Volt/Salt Typhoon pattern), Russian sabotage and hacktivism against European infrastructure, and Iranian retaliatory capacity make destructive scenarios — not just data loss — a planning baseline for anyone in or near critical supply chains. The EU response (NIS2 + CER/KRITIS-Dachgesetz, CSA 2.0, Cybersecurity Reserve, sovereignty requirements) will keep raising resilience expectations, and a Taiwan escalation would activate pre-positioned access at scale.

Position: prioritize resilience over marginal prevention — segmentation, offline-capable operations, tested recovery from destructive attack (incl. hypervisor and backup compromise), and supplier concentration mapping. Monitor sovereignty requirements where dependencies on US hyperscalers exist.
